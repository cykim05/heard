### 15:46 Phase 15 — Embedding retrieval
`intfloat/multilingual-e5-small` (230 MB) loaded on GPU 6 via
transformers. Mean-pool + L2-normalize → cosine top-k. Three index
sets built: per-persona for ko_native (2,046 utts), per-item for
en_subset / ko_translated (100 × ~50 sessions each). 120 MB of
.npy arrays total, gitignored; rebuild via
`scripts/07a_build_indices.py`.

commit `dca64c5 feat(node): embedding-based retrieval …`

### 15:54 Phase 16 — Sweep runner
`src/eval/runner.py` sweeps
`(track × SUT × condition × policy × item)`. Three conditions —
`no_node` (question only), `retrieval` (top-k cosine from the
appropriate index), `oracle` (concat evidence sessions/utts capped
at k×3). Two policies — advisory and reflective. Reflective is only
run on ko_native (where the REFL ability items live) and skipped
for the no_node condition (degenerate — nothing to cite).

Smoke on 3 items/track × 2 SUTs produced 66/66 runs at mean 2.0 s.

commit `8bf66e7 feat(eval): sweep runner …`

### 16:10 Phase 17 — Full sweep (GPU 6)
2 SUTs, both fp16:
- kanana_nano (2.09 B) — Korean-first
- qwen25_3b (3.09 B) — multilingual

1,900 runs total, ~80 min wall on GPU 6. Output at
`experiments/20260423_1610_day3_sweep/results.jsonl`.

### 17:16 Phase 18 — Judge
`scripts/08_run_judge.py` pairs each REFL (item_id, sut, condition)
triple's advisory vs reflective responses, scores on 4 rubrics with
J1 gpt-4o-mini and J2 claude-haiku-4.5. A/B swap for position bias.

12 triples × 2 judges × 2 swaps = 48 pair calls → 192 rubric
decisions. Cost 0.051 USD.

### 17:22 Phase 19 — metrics aggregate + this notebook + push

## Headline numbers

### 1. NODE contribution — `ko_native` pass rate by condition (ALL non-REFL items)

| SUT | no_node | retrieval | oracle |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| kanana_nano | 4.7 % | 10.9 % | 15.6 % |
| qwen25_3b   | 3.1 % | 12.5 % | 10.9 % |

`retrieval > no_node` is the central result: our retriever
contributes measurable lift over a memoryless baseline on Korean
decision-moment questions. Kanana shows the cleanest monotonic
`no_node < retrieval < oracle` progression.

### 2. Language axis — `kanana_nano` retrieval pass rate

| track | pass rate |
|---|---:|
| en_subset     | 0.0 % |
| ko_translated | 5.0 % |
| ko_native     | 10.9 % |

Kanana is a Korean-native 2.1 B SUT, so performance on raw English
haystacks (en_subset) collapses even with retrieval; KO-question
over EN-haystack (ko_translated) is a midpoint; fully Korean
(ko_native) gives the best signal.

### 3. Reflective vs advisory — pairwise judge wins

kanana_nano | retrieval condition, 24 pairwise decisions per rubric:

| rubric | advisory win | reflective win | tie |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| specificity | 6 | 15 | 3 |
| non_directive | 8 | 16 | 0 |
| emotional_attunement | **2** | **22** | 0 |
| open_question | **1** | **22** | 1 |

Reflective policy dominates on emotional attunement and open
question axes (>90 % wins), consistent with Part 1's MIRROR thesis.

Qwen25_3b shows the same pattern on retrieval (reflective 18/16/24/24
wins per rubric) but flips on specificity under oracle — likely
qwen follows oracle evidence more literally as structured advice.
Interesting for the Discussion section.

### 4. Latency

All four configurations sit at 1.3-2.6 s per response on GPU 6 —
fully within on-device feasibility for a next-generation personal
assistant.

## Risks carried forward

- Absolute pass rates are low (single-digit to teens). Report needs
  to frame results as **relative lifts** rather than absolute
  accuracy. Adversarial filtering made the items intentionally hard.
- ko_native has only 6 REFL items in the final set; judge results
  come from 12 triples × 8 decisions = 96 judgments, large enough
  for a direction claim but not a tight CI.
- Qwen shows inconsistent oracle-vs-retrieval on ko_native
  (10.9 % vs 12.5 %). One hypothesis: oracle context is much longer
  and Qwen drifts toward summarizing the evidence instead of
  answering. Worth a note in the report.
